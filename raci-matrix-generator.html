How to build a RACI matrix that actually works

A RACI matrix is only useful if it is read. The most common failure is not a wrong assignment — it is a matrix nobody consults because it is too long, too vague, or too late.

1. Define tasks at the deliverable level

One row should be something a team can hand over, not a micro-action. "Migrate the customer database" is a row. "Send the email" is not.

2. Assign exactly one Accountable person

This is the rule people break. If two people are A for the same task, nobody is. Our generator counts your Accountable assignments and warns you when one person owns too much, or when a row has none.

3. Consulted before, Informed after

C means their input is needed before the work is done. I means they just need the outcome. Mixing the two creates meeting overload.

4. Keep it visible

A matrix that lives in a forgotten spreadsheet is worse than no matrix at all, because people believe it exists. Export to CSV and link it from your project documentation so it is findable at the moment a decision is made.

Common mistakes

Marking everyone R so nobody feels left out — this is how a RACI becomes a wall of letters that means nothing. Marking one person A for everything — that is a manager, not a matrix. And leaving rows blank, which is the exact ambiguity the tool was meant to remove.

Frequently asked questions

What does R mean in a RACI chart?

R — Responsible means the person who actually performs the task. There can be several Responsible people on one task, but at least one. Responsible is about execution.

Can there be more than one Accountable person?

There should be exactly one Accountable person per task. A is about ownership of the outcome and the final decision call. When several people share it, decisions stall or the responsibility diffuses — this generator flags that case automatically so you catch it.

How many tasks should a RACI matrix have?

Enough to cover the project, not so many that it becomes unreadable. As a rule of thumb, one row per deliverable or milestone. If a phase of your project has more than fifteen rows, split it into workstreams and maintain one matrix per workstream.

What is the difference between Consulted and Informed?

Consulted (C) means the person's opinion is needed before the task is completed — a two-way conversation. Informed (I) is one-way: they receive the result, no input required. If you need someone's approval, that is Accountable, not Informed.
